    PROG and RESTART start lit. Key V05 N09 ENTR to see alarm 01107: the restart tables failed their check because erasable memory powered up blank, with no pad load. RSET clears the lamps.

    Both computers run the real flight software, executed instruction by instruction on an emulated AGC. Each one's clock is synced to mission time, so V16 N65 ENTR shows the elapsed time. Keyboard works too: digits, V, N, +/−, Enter, C (CLR), R (RSET), P (PRO).

    How this works

    The computers. Comanche 055 (Columbia) and Luminary 099 (Eagle) are assembled from the AGC source into core-rope images that match the flown ropes word for word. They run on Virtual AGC's yaAGC CPU emulator compiled to WebAssembly, at the real 85.3 kHz instruction rate. Every digit and lamp on the DSKY comes from the software writing its output channels.

    The flight. The trajectory is integrated in your browser when the page loads, in full 3D. It starts at the real liftoff time (13:32 UTC, 16 July 1969) from Pad 39A, using the Moon's and Sun's actual 1969 positions from published ephemeris formulas, Earth's rotation and oblateness (J2), and the Moon's orientation from the IAU model. Closed-loop guidance flies every powered phase with the real engines' thrust and specific impulse. The burns are targeted the way Mission Control did it: TLI to a 111 km pass behind the Moon on an orbit over Tranquility Base, PDI to the landing site's real coordinates, and TEI to the real entry time.

    Still simplified:

    • Rendezvous burns (CSI, CDH, TPI) are replaced by a smooth guided blend.
    • Midcourse corrections are not flown, and lunar gravity is a uniform sphere (no mascons).
    • Entry uses a fixed bank profile, so splashdown lands a few hundred km from the real point.
    • The guidance computers aren't connected to the simulated spacecraft's sensors; they run alongside.

    Imagery. Earth (Blue Marble, city lights, clouds) and Moon maps are NASA-derived textures from the three.js examples, placed with the real Earth rotation and lunar orientation.

    Kennedy. Launch Complex 39 is drawn as it stood in July 1969: the Vehicle Assembly Building (160 m tall, with no flag yet; that came in 1976), the Launch Control Center, the crawlerway, Pads 39A and 39B, the turning basin and the Operations & Checkout Building where the crew suited up. Building shapes and positions are simplified and approximate.

    Houston. The Manned Spacecraft Center (renamed Johnson Space Center in 1973) and the 1969 downtown skyline are simplified models. Building 30, Mission Control, is at its real coordinates; the other buildings, Clear Lake, Galveston Bay and the coastline are approximate.
